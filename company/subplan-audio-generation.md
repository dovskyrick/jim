# Subplan — Audio generation pipeline

_2026-10-23 · Status: LOOSE · Each subtopic about 70 words. Order: human steps → tests → implementation._

## Goal
Lessons with no clipped, cut-off or clicking audio, generated by a TTS service much cheaper than today's. The Android player waits: it is clunky but good enough. This subplan covers only `AudioGeneration/`: switching to a cheaper provider and engineering clean clips. Success means a Greek lesson that a native speaker can listen to from start to end without noticing a single join, at well under one dollar per course.

## Where the pipeline is today
The pipeline reads lesson scripts with `[pause]` markers and calls OpenAI `tts-1` for every quoted phrase (voice `alloy`, mp3). It caches clips that repeat (`vocab-manager`), adds silence and joins everything with ffmpeg into one mp3 per lesson. It then uploads to Firebase Storage and updates `manifest.json`. A cost tracker caps spending at €10. The "doctor" repairs silent gaps. The weak spots are short words and joins.

## Why short clips break
TTS models are trained on full sentences. Given a single word or syllable they rush it, cut the first consonant, add a breath, trail into noise, or use questioning intonation. Each clip also comes back with its own loudness and leading or trailing silence. Joined with ffmpeg, these mismatches become clicks, jumps in volume and uneven gaps. So the fix is mostly engineering around the model, not finding a perfect model.

## Clean-clip recipe (to test)
1. **Carrier sentence:** synthesize the word inside a natural sentence, then cut it out using word timestamps.
2. **Never bare syllables:** ask for slow, careful speech instead.
3. **Trim** leading and trailing silence with a fixed threshold.
4. **Normalize loudness** to one target (EBU R128, about −16 LUFS).
5. **Fade** 5–10 ms in and out at every cut.
6. **One format:** same sample rate, mono, encoded only once at the very end.

## Automatic quality check
Every clip gets checked before it's used:
- Transcribe it with Whisper and compare against the expected text.
- Check the duration: too short means clipped, too long means noise.
- Check the peaks: clipping or near-silence fails.

Failed clips are regenerated, up to three times, then flagged for a human. This extends the existing doctor. The number to track: the percentage of clips passing on the first try.

## Word timestamps decide a lot
The carrier-sentence trick needs to know exactly where each word starts and ends. Google returns timepoints for SSML `<mark>` tags, and Azure streams word-boundary events, so cutting is precise and free. OpenAI returns no timestamps, so we would have to align afterwards with Whisper, which costs extra and is less reliable. This could make Google or Azure the winner even when voice quality looks similar. Test it, don't assume.

## Provider-neutral design
One small interface: `synthesize(text, language, voice, settings)` returns raw WAV plus optional word timings. Each provider becomes one adapter file, so switching costs a config change. Cache clips under a hash of text, provider, voice and settings, so a regenerated or changed voice never mixes with old clips. Keep the instructor voice and the target-language voice separate, because the best provider may differ for each.

## Human steps — test material
1. Write about 20 Greek test items: single words, two-word phrases, a question, a long sentence, and one tricky item ("Γεια σου", "ευχαριστώ").
2. Find a native Greek speaker willing to listen to about ten minutes of audio. Even one person is enough at first.
3. Choose a single instructor voice you like.
4. Decide a quality bar: "no audible join in a full lesson" is a good one.
5. Tell me when you're ready.

## Test plan (after human steps)
1. Generate the 20 items with every provider, both bare and in carrier sentences.
2. Run the clean-clip recipe and the automatic check on all of them.
3. Make a blind listening sheet: you and the native speaker score each clip from 1 to 5.
4. Compare score, pass rate and cost per course.
5. Pick a winner per voice role.
6. Record the result as DECIDED in `underthetable_PLAN.md`.
